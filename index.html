Answer 1:

Most companies use cloud-based email and calendar services due to their scalability, accessibility, and cost-effectiveness. Cloud-based services allow employees to access their email and calendar from anywhere with an internet connection, facilitating remote work and collaboration. Additionally, these services often come with built-in security features, automatic updates, and maintenance handled by the provider, reducing the burden on internal IT teams. This enables companies to focus on their core business activities while ensuring reliable communication and scheduling tools for their workforce.

Answer 2:

A mid-size company WOULD utilize Glacier for high-volume data that they must keep for compliance or archival purposes but rarely expect to look at again. Examples of this type of data include old financial records, archived emails, historical business documents, or even recovery backups. A company WOULD NOT store any data that is dynamic, frequently accessed, or critical for immediate business operations. That data might be customer information, active sales records, active project files like word documents, or primary backups. Why or why not a company would use Glacier is mostly based on the cost and access time trade-offs. Glacier is very cost-effective for long-term storage, but it has longer retrieval times compared to other storage options, making it unsuitable for data that needs to be accessed quickly or frequently. In addition, pulling data from Glacier can incur higher fees. Other alternatives for mass cloud storage & mass backup might include Google Cloud Archive Storage, Azure Archive Storage, or Wasabi Hot Cloud Storage.

Deep Root Analytics / Republican National Committee Voter Leak:
A political data analytics firm representing the Republican National Committee inadvertently exposed the personal data of 198 million United States voters (roughly 61% of the U.S. population at the time). The exposed 1.1 Terabyte repository contained voter names, dates of birth, home addresses, phone numbers, and predictive profiles calculating voter stances on controversial political topics.
What Went Wrong:
The company configured an Amazon S3 bucket for unauthenticated public access, allowing anyone with the link to access the data without any authentication or authorization.

Accenture Corporate Data Leak:
Global IT consulting giant Accenture left four massive Amazon S3 buckets publicly accessible to the open internet. The buckets contained critical internal infrastructure files, including plaintext API credentials, master encryption keys, digital certificates, and customer account data belonging to major corporate entities
What Went Wrong:
Developers migrated internal development resources over to production environments without conducting an access control review. The default access policies on the production buckets were left set to allow anyone to read their entire contents without verifying an identity

You might maintain your own web server for any number of resons. You may be a hobbyist and want to experiment or play around and see how it works. You could also be a professional wanting to learn more about how it all works and develop your skills professionally. However, you may want to use SaaS/PaaS/IaaS if you require access to more powerful hardware, need to scale servers quickly, or you are running a business and don't want to deal with the overhead of maintaining your own server.
